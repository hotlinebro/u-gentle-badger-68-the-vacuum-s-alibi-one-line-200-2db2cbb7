# Candidates — The Vacuum's Alibi

Judge criteria, most important first: (1) a funny one-line excuse for a missed
spot, (2) a real laugh, not a cute dust observation, (3) deadpan machine voice,
a little smug about sensors / schedule / battery, (4) surprise — a turn or a
specific detail nobody expects, not a stock appliance joke, (5) tight, every
word earning its place, punch at the end. Plus uniqueness: byte-identical
entries share one rate and the earlier receipt places above its copies, so a
stock joke ("dust bunny", "I was on break") is poison even if it is funny.

## Shortlist (all one line, all under 200 characters)

1. **"The kids named the dust in that corner Gerald, and my sensors agree. I do not clean Geralds."** — PICK (92 chars)
   (1) clear excuse: the spot stays because it is Gerald. (2) real joke: dust with
   a name, then a machine rule that pluralises a proper noun. (3) deadpan, sensors
   name-checked, machine endorsing a child's naming. (4) "Gerald" is specific and
   unexpected; nobody's sensor data includes a first name. (5) setup, beat, punch
   ("I do not clean Geralds") lands last. Uniqueness: very high — the exact
   92-char string with this name and phrasing is near-impossible to duplicate.
2. "My predecessor died under the sofa in 2023, choked on a LEGO. I honour the fallen."
   Strong laugh and a great punch, very specific (LEGO, 2023), very unique — but
   no sensors / schedule / battery in it, so it gives away criterion (3).
3. "My battery hit 5% the moment I reached the spot. Coincidence? My logs say yes. My logs lie."
   Great twist (lying logs) and battery smugness, but the "coincidence?" setup is a
   familiar joke shape and the punch is a beat weaker than "I do not clean Geralds".
4. "I cleaned everywhere except that spot — perfection is a red flag, and I am not trying to get replaced."
   Smug self-preservation is a good machine voice, but it is long and the punch is
   a motive, not a laugh line.
5. "My sensors flagged that corner as emotional support dirt. I am not a therapist. I left it with its feelings."
   Surprising phrase ("emotional support dirt") and strong machine voice, but three
   beats is one too many and "feelings" softens the punch.
6. "I mapped the spot three times. Each time it moved 2 cm left. I do not negotiate with dust that relocates."
   Deadpan and specific (2 cm), but the joke is mostly the last clause and the
   setup is dry rather than funny.
7. "My map marks that corner 'do not disturb.' The dust hung the sign itself. I respect signage."
   Tight with a good punch, but closer to a cute observation than a real laugh.
8. "My map labels that corner 'here be dust.' I am a vacuum, not an explorer."
   Very tight, but the laugh is a smile, not a laugh — weak on (2).
9. "I did not miss the spot — I outsourced it to the broom, and the broom has been 'on its way' since March."
   Funny and specific (March), but the broom does the comic work, not the vacuum.
10. "That corner is where the Wi-Fi dies. I went in once. My map still has PTSD."
    Tight and relatable, but the punch leans on a tired acronym joke.

## Verdict

Candidate 1 wins on the judge's own order: it is the funniest excuse (1), it
lands a real laugh (2), it sounds like the machine talking about its sensors
(3), "Gerald" is the surprise (4), and the punch sits at the end with nothing
wasted (5) — while being the hardest line on the list to duplicate byte for byte.
